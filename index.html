Historical timeline

Key moments from early restrictions to present-day enforcement.

  1. Antiquity to 19th century

    Toxic smoke and poisons appear in sieges; the idea of banning “poison weapons” develops alongside the laws of war.

  2. 1899 and 1907

    The Hague Conventions restrict poison and asphyxiating gas projectiles.

  3. 1915–1918

    Large-scale use in World War I begins with chlorine at Ypres and spreads across the Western Front.

  4. 1925

    The Geneva Protocol prohibits the use of chemical and bacteriological weapons in war.

  5. 1935–1945

    Use in Ethiopia during the interwar period; stockpiles are built before World War II; poison gas is used in the Holocaust.

  6. 1960s–1988

    Use in Yemen and during the Iran–Iraq War, including the 1988 attack on Halabja.

  7. 1993–1997

    The Chemical Weapons Convention opens for signature in 1993 and enters into force in 1997; the OPCW is established.

  8. 1995

    Aum Shinrikyo attacks the Tokyo subway, a landmark case of non-state use.

  9. 2013–2018

    The Ghouta attack in Syria; Syria joins the Convention; OPCW–UN investigations; the 2018 Salisbury poisoning.

  10. Today

    Destruction of declared stockpiles, continued investigations, and debates over accountability. [Edit: add current developments.]

International law and treaties

What each instrument covers, and where it falls short.

TreatyYearWhat it coversLimitations
Hague Declaration and Conventions1899, 1907Restrict poison and asphyxiating gas projectiles in war.Narrow wording; no verification.
Geneva Protocol1925Prohibits the use of chemical and bacteriological weapons.Does not ban production or stockpiling; many states reserved a right of retaliation.
Chemical Weapons Convention1993 (in force 1997)Bans development, production, stockpiling, transfer, and use; requires destruction.Not universal in practice; attribution and enforcement remain contested.
